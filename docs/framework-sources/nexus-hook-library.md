## Stop the scroll before you sell the message.

A hook is the opening line, phrase or visual used at the beginning of a video, post or advert. Its job is to pre-qualify the right audience, create curiosity and give them a reason to keep watching. A strong hook does more than introduce the content. It dictates where the content goes next.

## Purpose and best practice

### Purpose

**Beat audience distraction.**

The opening seconds decide whether somebody stays or scrolls. The right hook makes the viewer feel the content is relevant before you ask for more of their attention.

### Best practice

**Write the hook first.**

Build the rest of the content around the promise, tension or question created by the opening. Never use a hook the content cannot deliver on.

## The 7 types of hooks

## 01. The Statement Hook

**Type:** Challenge

### How it works

A direct, bold observation aimed at a recognisable problem. It creates mild discomfort by describing the viewer's situation accurately, then implies that the content contains the reason or solution.

### Formula

> Observation + Mild Discomfort + Implied Answer

### Examples

1. If your content gets views but no enquiries, you're attracting attention from the wrong people.
2. If every sales call ends with “I'll think about it”, your offer isn't clear enough.
3. The reason your team feels busy but nothing moves is that nobody owns the outcome.
4. If your marketing only works while you're posting, you haven't built a system.
5. Most businesses don't need more leads. They need to stop wasting the ones they already have.
6. If customers keep asking the same questions, your buying journey is doing too little work.

## 02. The Label Hook

**Type:** Identity

### How it works

Opens with the exact role, industry or identity of the intended viewer. The relevant label cuts through surrounding noise because the audience immediately recognises that the message is for them.

### Formula

> [Target Audience or Title] + Call to Action or Value Proposition

### Examples

1. Estate agents, this is how you stop losing valuation leads after 5pm.
2. Gym owners, use this before spending another pound on ads.
3. Recruitment agency founders, your slowest process is costing you placements.
4. Coaches, here's a cleaner way to qualify leads before the sales call.
5. Local business owners, save this response for your next negative review.
6. Marketing agencies, this is the client-reporting workflow you're missing.

## 03. The Conditional Hook

**Type:** Qualify

### How it works

States a specific situation that the viewer must recognise in themselves. Everyone else is filtered out, while the ideal audience feels that the content was made around their exact problem.

### Formula

> If you are [Specific Person] in [Specific Situation], watch this.

### Examples

1. If you're generating leads but taking more than five minutes to reply, watch this.
2. If you're a service business with missed calls outside office hours, this is for you.
3. If your team copies customer details between three systems, save this.
4. If prospects visit your pricing page but don't book, try this.
5. If you own an agency and client reporting eats up Fridays, watch this.
6. If you have a strong offer but inconsistent follow-up, start here.

## 04. The Command Hook

**Type:** Action

### How it works

Gives a short, direct instruction linked to a clear risk, goal or outcome. The viewer instantly knows what action to take and why the next part of the content matters.

### Formula

> [Action Verb] + [Target Condition or Goal]

### Examples

1. Stop sending traffic to a page that doesn't answer this question.
2. Read this before hiring another salesperson.
3. Check these three numbers before increasing your ad budget.
4. Save this template for your next follow-up email.
5. Fix your response time before buying more leads.
6. Watch this before automating your entire workflow.

## 05. The List Hook

**Type:** Structure

### How it works

Promises a defined number of useful points, mistakes, ideas or steps. The number makes the content feel organised, finite and easier to consume.

### Formula

> [Specific Number] + [Desirable or Shocking Outcome]

### Examples

1. 7 reasons your leads stop replying after the first message.
2. 5 automations that save time without making your process worse.
3. 9 website mistakes costing local businesses enquiries.
4. 6 questions that expose a weak offer.
5. 4 follow-up messages for prospects who said “not yet”.
6. 11 ways to improve conversions without increasing ad spend.

## 06. The Story Opener Hook

**Type:** Tension

### How it works

Begins at the moment where the pressure, conflict or consequence is already visible. The background is explained afterwards, making the viewer stay to understand how the situation happened and what followed.

### Formula

> Start at Maximum Tension or Conflict + Explain Context Later

### Examples

1. The client had spent £8,000 on ads and couldn't tell me where a single lead had gone.
2. Ten minutes before launch, the booking form stopped sending enquiries.
3. I was halfway through the sales call when the prospect showed me the exact proposal our competitor had sent.
4. At 9:03 on Monday morning, 214 customer records disappeared from the CRM.
5. The campaign looked like a success until we opened the sales pipeline.
6. The phone rang for the seventh time while everyone in the office assumed somebody else would answer it.

## 07. The Exclamation Hook

**Type:** Emotion

### How it works

Leads with a strong reaction, surprise or sense of urgency. It should feel immediate and emotionally genuine, with the explanation arriving straight afterwards.

### Formula

> High Emotion or Reaction + Immediate Explanation

### Examples

1. This is completely backwards. Businesses are buying more leads before fixing their follow-up.
2. I cannot believe businesses are still doing this. They're making customers wait until Monday for a reply.
3. This one change cut the response time from hours to seconds.
4. We've been measuring the wrong thing. Views don't matter if nobody takes the next step.
5. This might be the most expensive “free” tool I've ever seen.
6. You need to see what happened after we removed half the workflow.
